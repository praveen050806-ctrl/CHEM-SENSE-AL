THE PROBLEM

A printed date is a guess, not a measurement.

Expiry dates assume ideal handling. In reality, temperature abuse, refrigeration failure and delivery delays change a product's actual condition well before — or sometimes well after — the date on the label.

Spoils early

A cold-chain break can spoil a product days before its printed date, with no visible warning to the consumer.

Discarded early

Retailers and shoppers throw out food that's still perfectly good, simply because the fixed date has passed.

THE QUESTION WE ASK TODAY
"Has the printed date passed?"
THE QUESTION CHEM-SENSE AI ASKS
"What is the actual condition of this product, right now?"

THE SOLUTION

Five layers turn a chemical reaction into a decision.

1

Smart chemistry

A calibrated indicator generates a measurable visual response as the product changes.

2

Smartphone camera

Existing consumer hardware captures the indicator — no dedicated device needed.

3

Computer vision

The AI layer extracts colour and feature data from the captured image.

4

Decision engine

The extracted signal becomes a dynamic freshness score and a clear verdict.

5

User interface

SAFE, WARNING or REJECT is shown as one simple, actionable result.

Smart Chemical Indicator → Camera → Image Processing → AI Model → Freshness Score → Action

ANTICIPATED QUESTIONS

AI Responce .

Q

Is this just a colour sensor?

No — it combines chemical sensing, smartphone computer vision and AI decision logic, with storage/QR links planned for the full system.

Q

Can it be low cost?

The indicator integrates into packaging while the consumer's existing smartphone provides the optical hardware, avoiding per-pack electronics.

Q

Does it certify food safety?

No. The prototype demonstrates the software/decision layer only — production use needs food-safety validation and certification.

Q

What is the innovation?

Turning packaging from a passive fixed-date label into a condition-aware interface with a dynamic freshness score.

SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY NOTE

This prototype demonstrates the software and decision layer only. It must not be presented as laboratory validation, or as proof that a colour guarantees food safety.
